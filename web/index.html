01 · The case

One process, five Earth-system components

Indonesia holds one of the largest tropical peat carbon stocks on Earth. When it's wet, peat doesn't burn. When it dries out, it can smoulder for weeks. Every link in that chain can be measured from orbit, so we treat it as a detective case: what is changing, where, by how much, and is it significant?

Why it matters

Peat fires are among the largest single carbon pulses on the planet in El Niño years, and the haze reaches tens of millions of people in Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore.

Experiment site

Sumatra and Indonesian Kalimantan (95–119° E, 6.5° S–6° N), with climate sampled at 10 peat-dome hotspots in Riau, Jambi, South Sumatra and West, Central and South Kalimantan.

What we compare

Before vs after the 2016 regime shift, El Niño vs neutral years, and 2027–2030 futures under different ocean pathways and governance levels.

07 · Research, data, analysis & methods

How we know what we claim

Research question

Has a decade of peat-fire governance broken the ocean → drought → fire → haze chain over Sumatra and Kalimantan, and what does the record 2026 El Niño imply for 2026–2030?

Hypotheses

  1. H1 (trend): fire-season detections show a significant monotonic trend over 2003–2025.
  2. H2 (mechanism): year-to-year variability is explained by a measurable chain: ENSO/IOD → rainfall → root-zone soil wetness → fire → aerosol.
  3. H3 (regime): after removing the climate signal, fire shows a structural break consistent with the post-2015 policy response (peat restoration agency, moratoria, stronger enforcement).
  4. H4 (geography): the trend is not uniform. Some regions decline while others hold or rise ("same driver, opposite signs").

Modeling approach

  • Domain: Indonesian Sumatra + Kalimantan, 95–119° E, 6.5° S–6° N, 0.25° grid for fire and 0.5° grid for trend tests.
  • Period: 2000–2025 observed (2003–2025 for statistics, full Terra+Aqua years) and 2026 near-real-time.
  • Unit of analysis: the Jul–Oct fire season (about 77% of all detections in the domain).
  • Model: a chain of four cross-validated regressions, a structural-break term, and bootstrap Monte Carlo over real ENSO analog sequences.
  • Scenarios: 2026 observed vs replay-2015 vs governance relapse vs La Niña, and 2027–2030 ocean pathways × governance × rainfall shift.

Expected outcomes & key figures

  1. Trend + significance board, plus the FDR-tested spatial trend map (what, where, how much).
  2. Climate-expected vs observed fire (the regime gap) with the Pettitt change point.
  3. A 2026 nowcast and a 2027–2030 probabilistic projection.

1 · Trend detection

Mann–Kendall statistic S = Σi<j sgn(xj − xi), with tie-corrected variance and the Hamed–Rao (1998) effective-sample-size correction for autocorrelated series. The magnitude is Sen's slope β = median((xj − xi)/(j − i)), reported per year and as % of the mean per decade. As a sensor check, the trend is repeated on Aqua-only and Terra-only counts.

2 · Where: field significance

Each 0.5° cell with ≥ 50 detections gets its own Mann–Kendall test. Hundreds of tests produce false positives by chance, so p-values are controlled with the Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery rate (Wilks 2016), α = 0.05.

3 · Change point

Pettitt (1979) test, on raw detections and on residuals of ln(fire) regressed on soil wetness. Testing the residual asks whether fire dropped beyond what weather explains.

4 · Lagged links

Monthly values are de-seasonalised (each month minus its 2003–2025 mean). We then compute Pearson correlations at 0–6 month lags along the chain.

Key results

    The fitted chain (2003–2025, n = 23)

    LOO R² = leave-one-year-out cross-validated skill. Fire is modelled in logs because it responds multiplicatively to drying. The regime term is a 0/1 indicator from the Pettitt break year; the slider's "governance level" scales it.

    2026 simulator

    Inputs propagate downstream: rain = a + b·ONI + c·DMI → soil = d + e·rain → ln F = f + g·soil + h·G → AOD = i + j·ln F. With "use 2026 observations" on, rain and soil wetness are the measured Jul–Oct 2026 values. Uncertainty is the 10–90% range of the fire residual. The map distributes the total over 0.25° cells using the observed spatial pattern of high- or low-fire years (2003–2025), weighted toward the post-2016 pattern by the governance level. The monthly animation uses the observed month profile.

    2027–2030 Monte Carlo

    4,000 runs. Each run draws one real ocean sequence (the four years after a historical El Niño, the climatological years, or repeated El Niño years). For every link it adds a residual resampled from that link's actual 2003–2025 residuals (non-parametric bootstrap), so the spread reflects real model error. Outputs are the median, the 10–90% band, and the probability of exceeding the 2019 season.

    Limitations

    • Detections ≠ burned area. MODIS counts active fires at overpass time and misses fires under thick smoke or cloud, and smouldering peat with low radiative power. Treat counts as an index.
    • Orbit drift. Terra and Aqua have drifted from their nominal overpass times since about 2020–2022. The Aqua-only trend (−52%/decade, p = 0.008) agrees with the combined one, but part of the late decline could be observational.
    • Reanalysis climate. MERRA-2 rainfall and soil wetness are model-assimilated fields at 0.5°, sampled at 10 points, not in-situ peat water tables.
    • Small n. 23 seasons. Coefficients are robust in cross-validation, but extreme inputs (e.g. ONI > 2) are near or beyond the fitted range.
    • Regime = attribution by elimination. The post-2016 drop is what remains after climate. It is consistent with policy, but could also include land-use change (already-burned or converted peat).

    What would make it stronger

    • MODIS MCD64A1 burned area and GFED5 emissions (Earthdata login) for hectares and CO₂.
    • SMAP L4 soil moisture and GRACE-FO water storage for a second, independent dryness signal.
    • TROPOMI CO and OMPS aerosol index for a smoke check independent of MERRA-2.
    • A peat-depth mask (e.g. national peat map) to separate peat from mineral-soil fire.
    • VIIRS 375 m fires (2012+) for small-fire sensitivity.
